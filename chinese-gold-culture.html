Gold in Chinese culture

In Chinese families gold marks the big moments: weddings, births, the new year and new beginnings. It's also how many families store wealth. Here's what each tradition means and how to take part in it well.

Last updated 2026-09-30

Wedding gold: 三金 & 五金

Many families give the bride a set of gold as part of the betrothal gifts. 三金 ("three golds") is typically a gold necklace, a gold ring and gold earrings. 五金 ("five golds") adds a bracelet or bangle and a pendant. Customs vary by region and family, and some add a gold anklet or a heavier bangle as a wealth statement.

龙凤镯 dragon-and-phoenix bangles

A matching pair of bangles, dragon for the groom's side and phoenix for the bride, symbolises a harmonious marriage. At Cantonese-style weddings (过大礼, 上头 ceremonies), relatives may place several bangles on the bride.

Newborns: the longevity lock 长命锁

Grandparents often give a baby a gold "longevity lock" pendant, sometimes with bracelets and anklets, to "lock in" a long, healthy life. The 100-day celebration (百日) and the first birthday are popular occasions.

Gold beans 金豆: the youth trend

Young buyers collect 1-gram gold beans in jars as a savings habit. It's cheap to start and satisfying to watch the jar grow. The World Gold Council found that 62% of Chinese aged 18–24 own gold jewellery, up from 37% in 2019. The catch: small pieces carry a high premium per gram.

Lunar New Year & Caishen Day 财神日

Gold sells strongly before the Spring Festival. The 5th day of the first lunar month welcomes the God of Wealth, and many shops open (and many buyers buy) "for luck". See this year's dates →

Zodiac gold

Each year's animal inspires gold charms and coins, and people buy their own animal in their "benming" year (本命年). Find your zodiac gold gift →

Why gold? The number and the metal

  • Permanence: gold doesn't tarnish. That fits 久 (9, "lasting").
  • Portability: historically, family wealth that could travel.
  • Purity as virtue: 足金 and 千足金 signal honesty and full value.
