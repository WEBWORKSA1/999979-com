What does 999979 mean?

Six digits, three layers of meaning: four-nines pure gold, gold's atomic number and the Chinese wish for things that 久久久久 last forever. The number itself is also prime.

1 · 9999: four-nines gold and "forever"

In the bullion world, 9999 (written 999.9) means gold that is 99.99% pure. The Shanghai Gold Exchange's flagship spot contract is literally called Au99.99. Four-nines gold is the standard for investment bars and many coins.

In Mandarin, nine (九, jiǔ) sounds like 久, "long-lasting". Four nines read as 久久久久, "forever and ever". That's why lovers give 99 or 999 roses. It's also why traditional wedding gifts of ¥9,999 or ¥99,999 bless a lasting marriage. Imperial architecture used nine too: the Forbidden City is said to hold 9,999½ rooms, and its gates carry 9 × 9 = 81 nails.

2 · 79: Au, the element of gold

Gold sits at atomic number 79 on the periodic table (symbol Au, from the Latin aurum). Put 9999 purity next to element 79 and you get "pure gold, the element": a name that's hard to forget.

Seven (七, qī) is also linked with 起 qǐ, "to rise", and with the romantic Qixi festival. Read as a phrase, 79 ≈ 起久, "rise, and last."

3 · A prime number

999,979 is prime. It can't be divided by anything except 1 and itself. It's one of only eight primes between 999,900 and 999,999 (999907, 999917, 999931, 999953, 999959, 999961, 999979, 999983), and the third-largest six-digit prime. Pure gold can't be broken into other elements, so the number and the metal share that quality.

In one line: 999979 = 久久久久 + 起久 = four-nines gold + element 79 = Forever Gold.
